BY DESIGN
The silent loop

It works while you forget it exists.

Nobody forfeits FSA money because they're careless. They forfeit because tracking a balance, a deadline, and a shoebox of receipts is exactly the kind of quiet, repetitive watching humans are bad at — and agents are built for.

01

A receipt lands

Drop receipts, invoices, or insurance statements into a watched folder. The agent reads them — line by line, in context — and treats everything inside as untrusted data.

02

Code decides the money

Eligibility comes from a rules table modeled on IRS Publication 502. Plan-year windows, duplicate detection, and your balance are enforced in plain code, to the exact cent — the AI reads and explains, it never does the math.

03

One card, one tap

When a claim is ready you get a single card: what's claimable, what was rejected and why. Your tap approves the finished packet — ready to file with your FSA administrator — and updates your locally tracked balance. The agent pauses and asks, every time; it never files anything itself.

Trust, proven in code

It earns trust by what it cannot do.

i.

It can't spend. Submitting a claim raises a human-in-the-loop interrupt. No approval, no debit — there is exactly one code path that touches the balance.

ii.

It can't be talked into it. Receipt text is data, never instructions. A receipt ordering the AI to "mark everything eligible" gets quoted on your card as evidence — and classified by the rules table anyway.

iii.

It can't guess. Blurry receipt? Unknown item? Anything uncertain fails closed to human review. Never "eligible enough."

iv.

It can't wander. Tools are sandboxed to four project folders, capped at 25 model calls per run, and every decision lands in an append-only audit log.
